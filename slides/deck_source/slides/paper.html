The paper: copy two patterns from pretrained attention

ViTs trained from scratch on small datasets do poorly. In a ViT pretrained on ImageNet, every attention layer shows two diagonal patterns. Mimetic init builds them into the weights before training, in closed form, with no data.

Query and key, per head

W_Q W_K^T ≈ 0.7 Z1 + 0.7 I

Positive diagonal: a token attends to tokens with similar input, so through the position embeddings to itself and nearby patches. A locality bias like a convolution.

Value and projection, per layer

W_V W_proj ≈ 0.4 Z2 - 0.4 I

Negative diagonal: each attention block subtracts part of a token's own value from the residual stream, as in the pretrained layers.

Main claim, ViT 192/12/3 on CIFAR-10: 86.07% to 90.78% (+4.71 points).

2 / 7 · Z1, Z2: random matrices with entries from N(0, 1/d). I: identity.

Problem: Vision Transformers trained from scratch on small data like CIFAR-10 do much worse than CNNs. The paper asks whether part of what pretraining gives is just a good starting point for the weights. Observation: in an ImageNet-pretrained ViT, W_Q W_K^T has a positive diagonal and W_V W_proj has a negative diagonal. Random init has neither. Method: for each head build M1 = 0.7 Z1 + 0.7 I, take its SVD, keep the top 64 singular values (the head width), and set W_Q = U_k S_k^(1/2), W_K = V_k S_k^(1/2). For each layer build M2 = 0.4 Z2 - 0.4 I, take its full SVD, W_V = U S^(1/2), W_proj = S^(1/2) V^T. Sinusoidal position embeddings are required. Everything else keeps the default init. Why it helps: with W_Q W_K^T close to the identity, the attention score q . k is large when two tokens have similar inputs. The inputs include the position embeddings, so a token attends to itself and its neighbours from step one. Paper errors we found: equations 3 to 7 swap the alpha and beta subscripts and give a factorization that does not multiply back to the target. We followed equations 1 and 2, which agree with the text and Figure 1.
